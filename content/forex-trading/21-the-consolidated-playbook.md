# The consolidated playbook

This chapter is the course assembled into one working order: the rules
that carry the most weight, the walkthrough from session start to
review, and the honest caveats about where all of this came from.

## The rules that carry the most weight

If nothing else from the course is implemented, implement these:

1. Risk management beats trade selection. Size from the stop; reward at
   least equal to risk. One bad trade must never be able to end the
   account.
2. Cut losses without hesitation. A hard stop in the market, no
   exceptions, no widening.
3. Win rate is nearly meaningless. Track expectancy in R; a 25% win
   rate with big winners is a fine business.
4. Filter by regime before looking for setups. If the higher timeframe
   is against you, don't trade it.
5. Never trade a tight range. Overlapping bars with no swings destroy
   beginners faster than anything else.
6. The stop goes where the thesis breaks, plus a margin of error. The
   market hunts visible levels.
7. Correlated pairs are one position. EUR/USD, USD/CHF, and GBP/USD
   long at once is one large dollar bet.
8. Never average down. Add only with-trend, only on a second
   independent setup.

## The walkthrough

One pass through a session, using everything:

1. Filter — regime (trend, value structure, your own equity curve),
   session quality, news calendar. If any is adverse, don't trade or
   size right down.
2. Setup — with-trend pullback, rejection fade, or channel breakout,
   with two independent reasons.
3. Entry — stop order, on the lower timeframe, market moving your way.
4. Stop — thesis-breaking level plus 20–40 pips of margin. Size pays
   for it, at 0.5–1% of equity.
5. Target — from the market, at least 1× risk. Half off at 2×, a
   quarter at 3×, runner trailed.
6. Journal — thesis and reasons written before the entry, frozen.
7. De-risk — size down on drawdown; stop entirely at −2% in a day, −5%
   in a week, −10% in a month.
8. Review — weekly, by expectancy in R, split by setup type, by
   whether the trade was profitable immediately, and by what you passed
   on.

## The never list

- Never average down.
- Never widen a stop.
- Never trade a tight range.
- Never take a trade expecting less than you risk.
- Never hold a loser hoping.
- Never set a profit target by date.
- Never watch the news during the session — read the reaction after.
- Never skip the calendar.
- Never revenge-trade the same market immediately after a loss.
- Never scale a setup up before 30 logged trades of evidence.

## The weekly review

The review is where the system improves, and it has a fixed agenda:
expectancy in R per setup family — not total profit; the share of
winners that were profitable within two bars; and the log of passed
trades, right and wrong. Then act on it: retire the worst setup,
resize the best one, adjust nothing in between without 30 trades of
evidence.

Expect the whole path to take about five years, and keep your job
while it does. The traders who arrived treated those years as
education; the ones who didn't mostly funded them.

## Claims, not validated results

The honest close. Of the trading records behind this material, only
one was audited by an outside firm; the rest are broker and manager
statements plus the authors' own review. Some sources are broker
affiliates whose structural teaching is sound but whose numbers —
volume shares, spread levels — are dated and need re-measuring. And
backtesting is its own trap: in a zero-sum game, a backtest that looks
great often found an edge that was never in the sample.

Treat every specific parameter in this course — channel lengths, pip
margins, session boundaries — as a starting hypothesis to test on your
own data, not as truth. The durable parts are the structure: risk
first, stops that never widen, expectancy over win rate, and the
review loop.
