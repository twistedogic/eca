# The setup catalog

Chapters 10 and 14 introduced the strategy families and the price-action
vocabulary. This chapter assembles the three specific setups that the
interviewed traders actually trade, in enough detail to recognise them
on a chart — and to know when each one is invalid.

## The with-trend pullback

In an uptrend, price pulls back to the moving average. The first touch
is often a false start; the **second attempt** — a double bottom at the
average, with the second dip holding the first — is the classic buy.
Traders call it a "high 2": the second pullback low in an up-leg. The
mirror in a downtrend is a "low 2", a double top at the average, and it
is the classic sell.

Third and fourth attempts remain tradable when they form wedges — three
pushes that each fall shorter — but each later attempt carries less
conviction than the one before. Bar counting needs swings to count; in
a tight, overlapping range there are no second attempts, only noise,
and the correct action is no trade at all.

## The rejection fade

The most mechanical entry sequence in this course, five steps:

1. A small value area forms on the lower timeframe.
2. A tail pierces the level — a rejection, strongest as a single-bar
   spike.
3. An initiative move away from the level — fast and forceful.
4. A responsive move back toward the level — slow and weak.
5. A second initiative move away. That is the entry.

> Enter on the second initiative move, never the first — there are many
> false breaks during **value development**, and step 3 is where they
> all look identical.

The fade is a short at the level on the second rejection: the market
has now twice refused to trade there, and the responsive buyers or
sellers who defended it the first time have been spent.

## Refining the channel breakout

Chapter 10's Donchian case study established the base system — enter on
a break of the 55-day channel, exit on a break of the 20-day. Three
refinements turn it from a breakout system into a filter system:

- **ADX filter.** Don't take the breakout if ADX is declining. This
  eliminates losers and rarely eliminates a profitable trade — a trend
  that can't push its own ADX up is not a trend yet.
- **Rejection rule.** If the 55-day channel was flat or declining for
  five or more days before the breakout, and price closes back below
  the breakout level on the day of the break or the day after, exit on
  that close. This removes about half of the ordinary stop-outs and
  cuts per-trade risk by roughly 80%.
- **Last-bar stop.** Place the stop at the low of the last bar still
  under the breakout level — about one third of the full channel risk.

When trouble comes, the tactics apply in order: rejection rule first,
then the last-bar stop, then the 20-day exit.

One more piece of judgement scales with the setup: the length of the
breakout measures the power of the move. A 52-week high requires a far
bigger shift in fundamentals than a 5-day high, so weight longer
breakouts more heavily than short ones.
