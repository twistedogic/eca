# Leverage and margin

**Leverage** lets you control a position larger than your account. A
50:1 ratio means one dollar of your margin controls fifty dollars of
currency. **Margin** is the money you post to hold that position.

> **Leverage** is not a strategy. It is a multiplier on whatever position
> size you chose — including a position size you cannot afford to be
> wrong about.

## The arithmetic

Leverage is simply notional value divided by margin. At 50:1, a $100,000
position requires $2,000 of margin. At 20:1 it requires $5,000. Those
numbers are the same whether the position is a good trade or a bad one;
leverage does not change the probability of being right, only the size of
the consequence.

A standard lot of EUR/USD on a $10,000 account at 50:1 needs $2,000 of
that account as margin. The same lot on a $2,000 account needs $2,000 of
margin — the whole account, leaving nothing for a margin call.

## Caps, and where to find yours

Most regulators cap the leverage a retail broker may offer, and the caps
differ by jurisdiction and by the pair. Rather than assume a number, find
yours: the **leverage cap** that applies to you is set by the regulator
your broker is registered with, and it is published in that regulator's
rules. Offshore brokers often advertise far more than regulated ones, and
often with fewer of the protections that come with them.


A cap is a ceiling on how much leverage a broker may offer, not a level you
are expected to use. Treating the cap as a target is the single most
common way new accounts die.

> A **leverage cap** is a ceiling on what a broker may offer, not a level
> you are expected to use.

## Margin calls and stop-outs

If equity falls toward the amount posted as margin, the broker issues a
**margin call**: add funds or reduce the position. If you do nothing, the
broker closes some or all of your positions at the worst possible moment.
Most platforms also have a **stop-out level**, slightly above the margin
call, where positions are closed automatically to protect the firm.

Note what that means: the mechanism that ends your account is a price
move against you, taken at a moment when liquidity is thin and fills are
poor.

## Effective leverage

The number that matters is your own. **Effective leverage** is your total
notional exposure divided by your account equity — not the ratio your
broker advertises.

A $1,000 account holding one standard lot of EUR/USD is running 100:1
effective leverage, whether the broker's limit is 50:1 or 500:1. No
platform will stop you from doing this. The margin call will.

> Effective leverage is notional exposure divided by account equity — and
> it is the number that ends accounts.

The fix is not a better broker. It is position sizing driven by your stop
loss, which is the subject of the next chapter.
